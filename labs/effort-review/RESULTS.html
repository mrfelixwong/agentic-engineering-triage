How students should route effort

Low: routine workFastest and cheapest. In this lab it blocked 4.9 of 8 risks.
Medium: one deeper passBlocked 5.5 of 8 at modest extra cost.
High: no measured win hereSame mean quality as medium, but slower and costlier. Do not pay for it on this task.
Max: high-consequence test designBlocked 7.7 of 8. Use when missed edge cases can harm customers or a release.

Rule: Start with the lowest setting that meets your measured quality bar. Raise effort when the task has real depth and the cost of a missed case is higher than the extra model time. Always run the tests; effort is not a guarantee.

Method and evidence boundary

Every run used a fresh temporary repository and fresh Claude process. The order was randomized. The correct implementation, exact prompt, model, tools, contracts, fixture hash, and mutation grader stayed fixed. Only effort changed.

Final gate: max needed to beat low by 20 points, win at least 7 of 10 matched repetitions, and have no worse validity or completion rate. Observed: +35.0 points, 10/10 wins, equal 10/10 validity, and equal 10/10 completion.

Do not generalize the exact numbers. This proves an effort effect on this task and model. It does not prove max is best for every coding task. Re-measure when the task, model, prompt, or tool access changes.
